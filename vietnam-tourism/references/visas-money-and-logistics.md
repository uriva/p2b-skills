## 1. Visas, Entry Forms & Border Rules (CRITICAL 2026)

### Official Vietnam e-Visa
- **Mandatory Pre-Approval**: Israeli citizens (and most foreign passport holders) must obtain a visa **before boarding** their flight to Vietnam. (Exception: flying directly into Phu Quoc Island internationally and staying solely on Phu Quoc for up to 30 days—if connecting domestically via Hanoi/HCMC or leaving the island, a regular e-Visa is required).
- **Official Government Portal**: `https://evisa.gov.vn/`
  - **Cost**: **$25 USD** (single entry) or **$50 USD** (multiple entry), valid for up to **90 days**.
  - **Processing Time**: **3 to 10 business days** (Saturday, Sunday, and Vietnamese public holidays like Tet do not count).
  - **When to Apply**: Submit at least **2.5 to 3 weeks before departure**. If immigration flags a photo or typo, the clock resets for another 3–10 business days after you submit the correction.
- **Crucial e-Visa Form Tips & Troubleshooting**:
  1. **Downloading the Actual Visa ("Get e-Visa")**: Do **not** arrive at the airport with only the payment receipt email! Once approved, log into the lookup page on `evisa.gov.vn` (preferable on a computer) and click **"Get e-Visa"** to download the official PDF visa, and print a copy.
  2. **"Low Quality Passport Data Page" / Photo Mismatch**: If the portal rejects your passport scan or gives a low match percentage, send the photos to yourself on WhatsApp first and upload the WhatsApp-compressed JPEG—this reliably resolves format/resolution rejections.
  3. **Entry Date & Buffer Days**: Set the visa start date to the **date you land in Vietnam** (not the date you depart Israel). Always add **3–5 extra buffer days** to your exit date—once an e-Visa is issued, dates **cannot** be edited (overstaying even 1 day due to a flight delay requires applying for a brand-new visa).
  4. **Entry & Exit Ports**: Your entry checkpoint must match where you land. Listing any major airport for exit is fine; exiting from a different international airport than originally written on the e-Visa is routinely permitted.
  5. **Name Changes (Newlyweds)**: Your e-Visa name must match your passport and flight ticket; if your passport has a previous maiden name in parentheses, include it as shown on the passport machine-readable zone.
  6. **Mandatory "Religion" & "First Hotel" Fields**: Vietnam tracks demographic data on entry forms; writing `Jewish`, `None`, or `Believe in God` for religion is completely fine. For the address in Vietnam, enter your first night's hotel (or the Hanoi Chabad House address if you haven't booked yet).
  7. **Temporary Passports (דרכון זמני)**: Accepted for e-Visa and entry provided the passport has **at least 6 months validity** from the date of entry.

### Mandatory 72-Hour Pre-Arrival Entry Form
- In addition to the e-Visa, travelers (including foreign/EU passport holders) must complete the free digital pre-arrival declaration within **72 hours prior to arrival** at the official portal:
  `https://prearrival.immigration.gov.vn/`

### Proof of Onward Travel (כרטיס יציאה)
- Airlines at check-in and Vietnamese immigration officers inspect proof of onward travel out of Vietnam **randomly (~50% of the time)**.
- **Important**: A return flight from Bangkok to Israel does **not** satisfy the requirement for Vietnam—you must show a ticket exiting Vietnam itself. Flexible backpackers without a fixed exit date use temporary onward ticket services (such as `onwardticket.com`) 24 hours before flying.

### Aviation & Connection Warnings
- **No Malaysia Connections**: Israeli passport holders **cannot** transit through Malaysia (Kuala Lumpur); airlines will deny boarding.
- **Thailand Connections (TDAC)**: If transiting through Bangkok where you pass immigration (even for a 2-hour self-transfer to Hanoi), you must fill out Thailand's digital arrival card (**TDAC**) before landing in Thailand on both outbound and return legs.
- **Airport Fast Track**: Generally unnecessary at Hanoi/HCMC (standard immigration lines usually take 10–20 minutes), unless you have a tight (<3h) domestic connection at Noi Bai Airport.

## 2. Money, ATMs & Currency Exchange

- **Cash is King**: While upscale hotels and malls accept cards, most local restaurants, street stalls, homestays, markets, and tour agencies require **Vietnamese Đồng (VND) in cash**, or charge a **3%–4% POS terminal fee** on credit card payments.
- **Zero-Fee ATMs (CRITICAL Money Saver)**:
  - **VPBank** is the #1 recommended ATM chain across Vietnam because it charges **0 VND local ATM fee** for foreign cards and allows withdrawing up to **10,000,000 VND** per transaction (twice daily). Found in Hanoi, Da Nang, Hoa Lu/Ninh Binh, and HCMC.
  - *VPBank Troubleshooting*: If a Visa card is declined at a VPBank ATM, select **"Savings"** (rather than Credit/Checking) when prompted for account type.
  - **TPBank** is the best backup ATM with zero or very low fees.
- **Exchanging US Dollars (Strict $100 Bill Rule)**:
  - **Best Exchange Locations**: **Gold and jewelry shops (חנויות תכשיטים וזהב)** in Hanoi's Old Quarter (e.g. Ha Trung Street), Sapa, and HCMC offer the best USD-to-VND exchange rates—significantly better than airport booths (exchange only a small amount at the airport if needed).
  - **The Crisp $100 Rule**: Bring **new-series $100 USD bills (with the blue 3D security ribbon)** that are **100% clean, uncreased, tear-free, and free of any ink stamps or tiny marks**. Vietnamese exchangers and hotels routinely reject bills with the slightest tear or stain, and give much worse rates (or refuse) $20 and $50 bills.

## 3. Transport & Getting Around Vietnam

### Ride-Hailing & City Apps
- **Grab**: The essential Uber-equivalent for cars, motorbikes, and food delivery in Hanoi, Da Nang, Hoi An, Da Lat, Phu Quoc, and HCMC. Always use Grab from airports (Hanoi Noi Bai → Old Quarter is ~250k–350k VND / ~$10–$14 USD; budget solo backpackers can also take the $2–$3 airport bus).
- **Green SM (Xanh SM)**: All-electric taxi and motorbike app (turquoise vehicles). Frequently **cheaper than Grab**, clean, and reliable (pay in cash VND).
- **Other Apps**: *Be*, *inDrive*, and *Bolt* also operate in major cities. (Note: **Sapa does not have Grab**; use local green electric taxis or private drivers).

### Intercity Buses, Limousines & Trains
- **Booking Platforms vs. Direct**: Use **12Go Asia** (`12go.asia`) or **Vexere** to check schedules and operators. **Pro Tip**: Look up the bus/train company name shown on 12Go (e.g. *G8*, *Sao Viet*, *Chapa Express*, *Sapaly*, *Livitrans*) and book directly on the company's website or through a local WhatsApp agent to save 20%–30% in platform fees and select exact seats.
- **Bus & Van Categories**:
  - **VIP Sleeper Cabin Bus**: Private or double curtained pods with near-flat beds, USB ports, and blankets. Recommended for overnight rides (Hanoi ↔ Sapa, Hanoi ↔ Ha Giang). Note: tall travelers (1.90m+) or those prone to motion sickness on winding mountain roads may prefer daytime **Limousine Vans** or **Overnight Trains**.
  - **Semi-Sleeper Bus**: Basic angled seats without curtains; avoid on long overnight trips if budget allows.
  - **Limousine Minivan (9–11 seats)**: Plush reclining leather seats; takes ~2–2.5h Hanoi ↔ Ninh Binh (~300,000 VND / ~$12 USD) or ~5.5–6h Hanoi ↔ Sapa via expressway. **Crucial Tip for Couples/Groups**: Explicitly confirm that your seats are **next to each other** when booking—vans often sell remaining single isolated seats across different rows.
- **Overnight Trains**: Smoother than buses for motion-sensitive travelers (4-berth or 2-berth VIP sleeper cabins between Hanoi and Lao Cai/Sapa, Dong Hoi/Phong Nha, or Da Nang/Hue).

### Domestic Flights
- **Routes Where Flying Wins**: Hanoi ↔ Da Nang (Hoi An), Da Nang ↔ Ho Chi Minh City, Hanoi/HCMC ↔ Phu Quoc, and Hanoi/HCMC ↔ Da Lat. Fares are often $35–$70 USD, making flying far better than a 15-hour sleeper bus.
- **Airlines**:
  - **Vietnam Airlines** & **Bamboo Airways** / **Sun Phu Quoc Airways**: Reliable, often include checked baggage.
  - **VietJet Air**: Popular low-cost carrier, but **strictly enforces the 7 kg carry-on limit** at the gate. When booking on `vietjetair.com` (use the official `.com` site, not regional sub-sites), upgrading to the fare tier that includes a 20 kg checked bag often costs almost the same as the base ticket.
- **Airport Arrival Buffer**: Arrive **2 hours before domestic flights** and **2.5–3 hours before international flights**.

### Self-Driving Scooters & Cars
- **Scooters**: Require an Israeli motorcycle license matching the engine CC **plus** a physical International Driving Permit (IDP booklet) for travel insurance validity. Never leave your physical passport as a rental deposit, and photograph all existing scratches before riding.
- **Electric Scooters (E-Bikes)**: Available in Tam Coc/Ninh Binh, Cat Ba, and Hoi An (~150k–200k VND/day); **no license required**.
- **Car Rental**: Self-drive car rental is rare and strongly discouraged due to chaotic scooter traffic and strict liability laws; always rent a **car with a private driver** (~$35–$60/day depending on route).

## 4. SIM Cards, Tipping, Kosher, Medical & Daily Practicalities

### SIM & eSIM
- **Local Carrier**: **Viettel** is the #1 mobile network with the strongest 4G/5G coverage in remote mountains (Ha Giang, Sapa) and bays, followed by **Mobifone**. Avoid *Vinaphone* in remote areas.
- **Physical SIM vs. eSIM**: You can buy a physical Viettel SIM at the airport (~10–15 NIS more than in town, worth the convenience—verify the card actually says **Viettel** before leaving the counter) or get an official **Viettel eSIM** in 5 minutes at any official red Viettel store in Hanoi (~100 NIS/month). (Note: local numbers sometimes receive spam calls—simply ignore them). International eSIMs like **Airalo** also work reliably across Vietnam.

### Tipping Etiquette
- Street food stalls, casual restaurants, and Grab rides do **not** expect tips.
- For guided tours and multi-day drivers, tipping is customary and appreciated:
  - **Ha Giang Loop Easy Rider**: **300,000–500,000 VND total** (~$12–$20 USD) per rider at the end of a 3-day loop (~$5–$8/day).
  - **Private Tour Guide (Full Day)**: ~$10–$15 USD per day per group (or ~$5/person).
  - **Private Car Driver (Full Day)**: ~$5–$10 USD per day.

### Kosher, Shabbat & Religious Travelers
- **Chabad Houses (בתי חב"ד)**: Active centers in **Hanoi** (breakfast opens ~08:30), **Sapa**, **Hoi An**, and **Ho Chi Minh City**, with kosher meal deliveries available to **Tam Coc / Ninh Binh**.
- **Shabbat & Ha Giang Loop Logistics**: If starting the Ha Giang Loop on Sunday, spend Shabbat comfortably at Chabad in Hanoi (or Sapa) rather than in Ha Giang town, and take the Saturday night (*Motzei Shabbat*) VIP sleeper bus to Ha Giang.
- **Self-Cooking & Supermarkets**: Use the **Zekasher** website/barcode scanner in Vietnamese supermarkets (e.g., imported Australian UHT milk is kosher). If you plan to cook kosher food on the road, bring a compact travel pot/pan from Israel, as kitchenware is hard to find in tourist towns.

### Laundry, Barbers, Shopping & Electricity
- **Laundry**: Fast, cheap (~25,000–40,000 VND / ~$1–$1.50 per kg), and returned folded within 24 hours. Vetted spots: **Cu4 Laundry Hanoi** (`+84 36 441 9055` — 25k/kg with free hotel pickup & drop-off), **Hai Hanh Laundry** in Tam Coc (`+84 33 445 5687`), Sapa Laundry (`+84 97 672 37 09`), and **Cat Ba Laundry Shop**.
- **Barbers & Nail Salons**: *Hanoi Fresh Cut*, *Ducky Barber* (Hanoi), *Jay Barbershop* (Sapa), *The5 Barbershop* (HCMC). **Golden Rule**: Always show the barber a photo of yourself right after a haircut so nothing gets lost in translation! Recommended nail salons in Hanoi: *Nail Room 6* and *Fedi Nails*.
- **Shopping**:
  - Trekking gear, rain jackets, and fleeces are sold everywhere in Hanoi's Old Quarter and Sapa (slightly cheaper in Sapa than Hanoi; typical sneaker prices in Hanoi markets after bargaining are 350k–600k VND, and large 30–35kg suitcases cost ~700k VND).
  - Factory overstock of **Lululemon** can be found at *Wanderlust Outfit* in Hanoi. Malls: *Lotte Mall West Lake* and *Aeon Mall* (note: *Uniqlo* in Vietnam costs the same as in Israel/Europe).
  - **Luggage Shipping**: Travelers who shop in Hoi An or Hanoi can ship parcels directly back to Israel (e.g. via Delivereast `+972 54-348-8689`) or between Vietnamese cities so they don't haul extra weight on domestic flights.
- **Electrical Outlets**: Vietnamese sockets accept standard **Israeli 2-pin plugs** without an adapter. If you have a 3-prong grounded Israeli plug (like a laptop charger), bring a simple **3-to-2 pin adapter**.
- **Medical, Allergies & Drones**:
  - **Medical Care**: **Family Medical Practice** in Hanoi provides high-standard Western medical care (and has an Israeli doctor on staff). For stomach bugs, stay hydrated with electrolytes available at any pharmacy.
  - **Severe Allergies Warning**: **EpiPens are NOT sold in Vietnam.** Travelers with severe food/nut allergies must bring multiple EpiPens from Israel and carry a Vietnamese translation allergy card.
  - **Satellite Communicators (Garmin InReach / Magnus)**: Permitted, though travelers should check current Magnus declaration guidelines for customs.
  - **Drones**: Regulations for sub-250g drones were eased in 2026 at scenic nature sites, but flying near military, government, or dense urban zones remains strictly prohibited.
